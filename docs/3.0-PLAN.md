# Rescue HQ 3.0 — review and plan

Four independent reviews of the 2.0 "Aurora" app at `/dev/`: a family/UX
walkthrough, an engineering audit, an art-direction review, and a
layout/accessibility review. Findings were verified against the running app in
a headless browser at 320×568, 390×844 and 430×932 before anything was changed.

The one-line summary: **2.0 is a beautiful app with an unsafe foundation.** The
art direction, the letter helper, and the four different physical expressions of
"slow down" in the Calm Den are genuinely excellent. Underneath them, the app
could lose a night of a child's recorded voice to a single mis-tap, could not
work offline at all, and could permanently wedge the family's shared mailbox
with one long voice reply.

3.0 fixes the foundation and leaves the good parts alone.

---

## What shipped in 3.0

### Data safety

**The bedtime check-in autosaves.** `dev/js/diary.js`

Every answer was held in a module-level array and written only at the very end.
`exit()` discarded the take in flight and saved nothing; `enter()` wiped the
array. Tapping the back arrow at question 19 of 20 — or an iOS tab eviction, or
a flat battery — destroyed the whole session silently. (The author knew the
session was fragile: `login.js` already exempts an in-progress check-in from the
relock reload. Only that one loss path was covered.)

Now each recording is written to the day record as its `onstop` fires, the
selfie saves on capture, and the question index persists. Re-entering resumes
where she left off. A completed day clears the resume marker so it can never be
half-overwritten. Verified in `tools/test-data-safety.js`.

**Writes fail loudly.** `dev/js/store.js`

Every store method ended in `.catch(() => null)`, so a full disk was
indistinguishable from a successful save — and `finish()` painted the goodnight
moon *before* the write. Failures now raise a visible banner, quota exhaustion
says so specifically, and `navigator.storage.persist()` asks iOS not to evict
the archive. Writes also resolve on `transaction.oncomplete` rather than
`request.onsuccess`, so a rolled-back transaction is no longer reported as
saved, and same-key read-modify-writes are serialised so two concurrent
`updateDay` calls can't lose one patch.

**The v1 → 2.0 migration is non-destructive.** `dev/js/store.js`

The import did a blind `put` of every v1 row over the 2.0 database, guarded only
by a **localStorage** flag. iOS evicts localStorage independently of IndexedDB —
the normal asymmetry, since IndexedDB is the "important" store — so losing the
flag re-ran the import and a v1 copy of a letter (no replies, unopened) would
overwrite the 2.0 copy carrying her voice replies. The flag now lives in
IndexedDB beside the data it guards, and the import skips any key that already
exists.

**Backup.** `dev/js/backup.js`, new

There was no export of any kind. The badge editor now has *Save a copy of
everything*, which writes every day record, recording, selfie and letter to a
single dated JSON file, and shows how much the app is holding. This is the
family's only lifeboat and it makes every future migration recoverable.

### The sync wedge

`dev/js/mailsync.js`

A pantry basket tops out near 1.4 MB. A 60-second voice reply is ~0.5–1 MB of
AAC and base64 adds a third. When one exceeded the limit, `putBasket` threw —
and because bodies are uploaded *before* the index, the index write below never
ran and `monitorPass()` never ran either. The same doomed upload was then
retried every 90 seconds, on every device, forever: **mail and monitor sync both
dead for the whole family until someone found and deleted that letter.**

Oversized bodies are now skipped and flagged rather than thrown, a failed body
no longer abandons the index write (references to anything that failed are
stripped so the index never points at a missing basket), and deleting a letter
now issues `DELETE` for its photo and reply baskets — previously the child's
voice recordings stayed readable on a public relay forever after the parent
believed they were deleted.

### Offline

`dev/sw.js` + `tools/gen-precache.js`, both new

2.0 registered no service worker, deliberately, so dev builds were always fresh.
That broke the app in the one situation it exists for. It was worse than simply
being online-only: the v1 worker at `../` has scope over `/dev/`, so an offline
navigation here was answered with **v1's HTML**, whose relative asset paths then
resolved under `dev/` and 404'd — a white, unstyled page.

3.0 registers a worker at this scope, which takes those navigations back.
Freshness is preserved differently: the cache name embeds the deploy SHA, so
each deploy installs a new cache and drops the old. The precache manifest is
**generated from the tree** by `tools/gen-precache.js` in CI rather than
hand-maintained — v1's was a hand-edited list, which is a footgun for a file
whose only job is to be complete.

Verified: 35 files precached, and a full offline reload gives a working, styled
app with all 12 screens and zero errors.

### Graphics and UI

- **The check-in got a world.** It was the only child-facing screen with no
  painted scene — a flat gradient with the app's last surviving v1 sprite on
  it, on the app's most tender surface. It now has the meadow at moonrise and
  Nimbus, the same character as the breathing moon, curled asleep with a rim
  light from the moon. `js/pups.js` and the three v1 pup SVGs are deleted; they
  existed only to set that one image.
- **Mission hints fit.** `.mission-hint` was `white-space: nowrap`; the bridge
  hint measured **395px inside a 390px viewport** and bled off both edges on
  every phone. The two newest missions already carried the fix locally — it is
  now global.
- **Mission chrome no longer eats taps.** The title and hint pills sat at
  `z-index: 9` over the play surfaces with default pointer events, so a finger
  landing on them did nothing. On the beacon the hint reads *"Hold anywhere"*
  while being the one place holding was ignored.
- **Top Pup day no longer breaks the hub.** `#screen-hub > *` (specificity
  1-0-0) beat `.sparkle` (0-1-0), so the eight celebration sparkles became
  in-flow, non-shrinking flex items and added ~168px to the column — pushing the
  nav off-screen on the happiest day the app can produce.
- **Press feedback.** Tap highlight is suppressed globally and almost nothing
  had an `:active` state, so a tap produced no response until the 600ms screen
  fade. For a child with imprecise aim, "did it hear me?" is the whole question.
- **A single tap on a monitor box answers her.** `wiggle()` was written for
  exactly this and never called — ESLint found it independently. A big glowing
  button silently ignoring taps is now a gentle wiggle.
- **Reduced motion actually works.** The existing block covered 11 selectors and
  missed the pulsing banners, the unread-mail glow and the firefly halo. It also
  could never stop the star field: 137 stars carry `style="animation:twinkle…"`
  inline, which a plain rule cannot beat. It now uses `!important`.
- **Contrast and tap targets.** Five computed WCAG failures fixed
  (worst: 2.24:1). `#badge-edit` was 34×34 and `.cal-day` 36.9×36.9 at 320px,
  both under the 44pt minimum. Focus rings added — five inputs set
  `outline: none` and nothing replaced it.
- **Scroll safety nets** on the hub and the check-in. Both could overflow with
  no way to scroll: the hub with both banners up on 390/393 (the compact
  breakpoint is `max-height: 700px`, so it fires on the small phones that
  already fit and never on the tall ones that break), and the check-in on the
  selfie step at 320/375, which stranded her at the last question.

### Performance

- `sky.js` allocated **25 offscreen 128×128 canvases (~1.5 MiB) at every
  launch** — plus ~10 MiB of backing store on first resize — for a screen the
  child may never open. Now built on first entry.
- **~200 CSS animations ran continuously, of which at most ~60 were ever
  visible**, because all 12 screens stay in the DOM and `visibility: hidden`
  does not stop animation timelines. Inactive screens are now paused.
- The **login screen ran an idle 60fps rAF loop from launch** — unconditionally
  rescheduling itself with nothing to do, against a wake lock that stops the
  browser throttling it. It now runs only while a finger is down.
- `backdrop-filter: blur(2px)` behind a 72%-opaque panel is invisible but forced
  iOS to read back the animated aurora for three hub panels every frame.
- The beacon ran `querySelectorAll` 60×/second for state that changes ~4× a
  round.

### Tooling

There were no tests, no linting and no CI checks beyond "did the deploy action
finish" — which succeeds even if every JS file has a syntax error.

| Tool | What it catches |
|---|---|
| `eslint .` | `no-undef` across the implicit global-module architecture. 0 errors. |
| `tools/check-dom-contract.js` | Every `getElementById` literal exists in the HTML. Runs in CI. |
| `tools/smoke.js` | All screens open, no page errors, no overflow, hints fit, hub nav reachable. |
| `tools/test-data-safety.js` | Check-in autosave/resume, failure banner, day keys, backup. |
| `tools/gen-precache.js` | Regenerates the offline manifest. Runs in CI. |

`check-dom-contract` found a real dead reference on its first run.

## Not done, and why

### 1. The shared mailbox is public — highest priority for 3.1

`dev/js/mailsync.js` hardcodes the pantry ID, and that file is served publicly.
getpantry.cloud uses the ID as its **only** credential, so anyone who views
source can read *and write* the store. It holds the child's first name, her
recorded voice, the "from" photos, and a 120-day log of her behavioural marks.
The letter templates are explicitly designed to elicit specific incidents and
weave them into the text verbatim.

Realistically, nobody is enumerating 122-bit UUIDs — the exposure is that the ID
is in a public repo and in View Source. **The concrete risk is not exfiltration,
it is injection:** the store is world-writable, so anyone with the ID can put
arbitrary text in front of a four-year-old inside a trusted "letter from Ryder",
read aloud in the app's own voice.

3.0 fixes the part that could be fixed without changing the family's setup: the
tombstone path now deletes remote media. The real fix is client-side envelope
encryption (WebCrypto AES-GCM from a passphrase the parent enters once per
device) plus an HMAC on the index so unsigned documents are rejected — that
closes the injection path, which is the one that reaches the child. It is
deliberately **not** bundled here: it changes the setup flow on every device and
needs a key-distribution decision, and it should not ship in the same release as
the storage-layer changes above. Rotate the pantry ID as part of it.

The README previously stated that letters "live only on the phone". That was
true of v1 and false of 2.0. It has been corrected.

### 2. Zero-padded day keys

Keys are `YYYY-M-D`, unpadded, so they sort lexically wrong (`2026-10-1` before
`2026-2-1`). Nothing depends on that ordering today, which is why 3.0
**centralised** the logic in `dev/js/day.js` — with a rollover timer, fixing a
monitor that kept writing to yesterday if the phone sat on the hub past midnight
— but deliberately kept the existing format. Changing it orphans every day
record already on the family's phones. It needs its own release with a one-time
key-rewrite migration, done *after* the backup shipped here exists, so it is
recoverable.

### 3. The v1 / 3.0 fork

91% of root's CSS selectors also exist in `dev/`, but only 31% of the shared
ones agree — near-total overlap with majority divergence, and no way to tell
whether a given difference is an intentional 2.0 redesign or a v1 fix never
forward-ported. `dev/assets/` is a byte-identical 112 KB copy of `assets/`.
Nothing on this list is durable while two forks exist. The move is to promote
`dev/` to root once 3.0 has run on a real phone for a while; it is not a
same-release change because v1's `calmpups` database must be migrated first.

### 4. Art consolidation

The world is described 12 times instead of once: 11 hand-typed sky gradients,
198 distinct hex values (72% in near-duplicate clusters), five incompatible glow
techniques, and four of five pups sharing byte-identical vector geometry. The
recommended sequence is a shared `<defs>` block, a real token layer that SVG can
consume via utility classes, one additive light model ported from `sky.js`
(which already does it correctly), and rim lights on the pups. All consolidation
— none of it requires a new drawing.

### 5. Product ideas worth considering

From the family walkthrough, in rough value order:

- **"How do you feel?" on the hub.** The app measures her and interviews her,
  but nowhere can she say *"I feel bad right now"*, and nothing routes a feeling
  to a mission. The Calm Den is a toy chest, not a first-aid kit.
- **Repair for the Daily Monitor.** Three strikes shows a red banner naming her,
  and there is no way back — no path where a completed calm mission clears one.
  In a regulation app the whole lesson is rupture *and repair*.
- **Read the questions aloud.** `Mail.speak()` already exists and works well;
  the check-in uses none of it, so bedtime requires a parent with the energy to
  read twenty questions.
- **Let her hear herself**, and **let HQ answer her voice replies** — the sync
  layer already carries them; nothing tells the parent one arrived.
- **Persist her paintings.** Paint the Sky is a real sandbox and the best-feeling
  code in the repo, and it is erased every time she leaves.

## Do not break these

Verified as genuinely good, and load-bearing:

- `dev/js/mailtemplates.js` — seven letter types with randomised sentence banks,
  parent fragments woven mid-sentence, and real developmental discipline
  (behaviour-not-child, effort-over-outcome, always ending in belief).
- Slowness as the mechanic, four different ways: sustained hold (beacon), slow
  drag (bridge), slow-brush-is-wide-brush (sky), stillness (fireflies). None of
  it named or explained.
- No fail states. Nothing punishes; the bridge just wobbles.
- The letter as a physical object — wax seal, ruled parchment, the slide-in.
- The "say it like" phonetic field, so the reader pronounces her name right.
- The scars: iOS audio re-armed on every `pointerdown`, the sentence-chunked TTS
  queue, building the diary from `QUESTIONS` rather than the sparse `answers`
  array, and `deleted`-beats-alive in the sync merge. Each is a real bug already
  paid for once.
